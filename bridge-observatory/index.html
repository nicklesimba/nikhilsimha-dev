Load Posting Compliance Observatory

A camera-only screening instrument for load-posted bridges. It answers one question nobody currently sells an answer to: is this bridge's posted weight limit being obeyed, how often is it not, and can the owner prove it?

Built to be wrong out loud rather than confident in private. When it cannot decide, it says so, and the count of times it could not decide is published as prominently as the violations.

Why it exists

About 63,000 of the roughly 623,000 bridges in the United States are posted below legal loads, and five out of six of those are owned by counties and towns rather than states. Measuring whether anyone obeys those postings is solved only at the very top of the market: a weigh-in-motion sensor array runs into millions of dollars per bridge. Everywhere else the state of the art is a sign, and a hope.

The gap is not detection, which is commoditized, and it is not weight measurement, which has better funded teams. The gap is that nobody joins a cheap camera to the bridge's own federal record and reports compliance per structure, at inventory scale, for the long tail of local bridges.

Prior art, stated plainly. Camera-triggered monitoring at a weight-restricted bridge is not new. The Cawood Swing Bridge in the UK runs number-plate cameras triggered by vehicle length for exactly this purpose. That is one bespoke installation, plate-centric, using length as a proxy, with no inventory join and no replication path. It is evidence the mechanism works and that someone paid for it once. What remains unoccupied is the inventory-scale, federally-joined, privacy-minimal, abstention-honest version for the US long tail.

Disclaimers carried by the tool itself

    The honesty rules

    These are the design, not the disclaimer. They are enforced in code and in tests.

    Never estimate weight from pixels

    Structural facts come from federal data and from the sign. Nothing about a bridge or a vehicle's mass is inferred from imagery.

    Flag only on a lower bound

    A crossing is called a violation only when the vehicle class's documented minimum curb weight, the lightest that class can possibly be when empty, already exceeds the posted limit. A transit bus exceeds a three ton posting carrying nobody. That makes every violation a conservative certificate rather than an estimate. Averages are barred by an automated test, because an average is not a lower bound.

    Abstain, loudly

    Where the class minimum does not settle the question, the answer is ABSTAIN, and abstentions are first-class ledger rows with their reasons. A compliance rate published without its abstention count would be dishonest, so the code refuses to produce one: every rate is emitted next to the denominator it was computed over.

    Never claim legality

    There is no CLEAR verdict. Asserting a vehicle is within the limit would require a class maximum, which is not citable: a manufacturer's gross weight rating says nothing about the mass of a vehicle on the day, and an overloaded vehicle is precisely the population a posting cares about. The verdict space is two-valued on purpose.

    Screening, not enforcement

    Not a citation system and not a weight measurement. Ticketing requires certified sensors under the law. This produces evidence, statistics and a trend line for the bridge owner.

    Privacy is a feature

    No plate recognition. Evidence is kept only for certificate-grade exceedances, and plates in saved evidence are blurred by default. The limits of that blurring are documented rather than glossed.

    What measurement did to the first version

    This grew out of a simpler tool, a load risk visualizer that tints each vehicle by its class's typical weight. That answers a softer question and answers it well. This one exists because class-typical weights, perfectly reasonable for a visualization, are not good enough to accuse anyone of anything.

    measurement forced

    • Curb-weight floors, cited, averages banned by test
    • The freedom to abstain, no CLEAR verdict at all
    • Crossing events, since you cannot count violations before vehicles
    • A labeled test set, because it was not
    • The sign, since a rating never bound a driver
    • An auditable ledger

    The measurement that reframed the project

    On 134 hand-labeled crops from our own footage, the raw detector called five objects buses. None of them were buses. Two were the Golden Gate Bridge's own tower and railing. Mapping raw detector labels onto weight classes would have produced a 35.8% false accusation rate. The refinement layer drops that to zero false heavy-vehicle accusations, at the cost of abstaining on roughly half of all vehicles.

    Honest current state

    The pipeline is built end to end and is honest end to end. It is not ready to make a real-world claim, for one reason, and the reason is data rather than code.

    The compliance model can currently decide two vehicle classes, transit bus and articulated truck, because those are the two with defensible published curb-weight floors. The vision layer cannot yet validly produce either one: the footage contains no buses at all, so that class has zero validation samples, and no available signal separates an articulated truck from a single-unit truck, so that class is never emitted. The certificate chain therefore has no validated basis end to end, and the correct output today is abstention, which is what you see above.

    What unblocks it is footage of a heavy vehicle on a genuinely load-posted bridge, which is the rural and locally owned scenario that stock footage of famous bridges structurally cannot provide. That is a pilot camera, not a code change. A fine-tune plan is prepared and deliberately not launched, and its binding acceptance criterion needs that same footage.
